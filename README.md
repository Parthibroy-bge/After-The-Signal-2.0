**Summary**

"**After the Signal**" is an interactive, game-like educational interface built by Team Moon Knight for the NASA International Space Apps Challenge to investigate NASA’s silent and abandoned missions like Viking I & II and Phoenix. It helps students and educators explore authentic planetary datasets while teaching critical thinking to distinguish between planetary habitability and actual biological life. The platform operates through an intuitive learning storyline and vibe-coded prototype that breaks down historical astrobiology experiments and open-access space archives.

**Core Concept & Purpose**

When a space mission goes silent, does its scientific legacy end?

"After the Signal" addresses this question by providing a fun, intuitive, game-like interface (mainly focusing on the Viking Archives) that helps students and educators think like scientists and learn about NASA’s now-abandoned Viking I & II missions, as well as the scientific reasoning and data behind astrobiology experiments.What makes our project uniquely creative is that it transforms dry historical archives into an immersive, gamified simulation, re-framing 'abandoned' space equipment not as dead ends, but as active virtual laboratories for modern student discovery."

**Featured NASA Missions**

1. Viking 1 & 2 (Mars Landers, 1976–1982)

       Conducted the first biological life experiments on Mars.

       Looked for active organic metabolisms.

       Results remained inconclusive.

2. Phoenix (Mars Arctic Lander, 2008)

       Directly discovered water ice in Martian soil.

       Detected perchlorate soil chemistry.

       Mission ended after the Martian winter.

**Scientific Methodology & Key Learnings:**

1`.Habitability vs. Life: The discovery of water ice by Phoenix confirms habitability potential, not biological presence. Water routinely exists throughout the solar system without biological processes (abiotic water existence).

2.Multiple Biosignatures Required: True science requires multiple corroborative lines of evidence—such as organic compounds, structural fossils, or active metabolic traces—rather than single indicators.

**Why It Matters:**

1.Builds Scientific Curiosity: Engages students with active, unsolved planetary mysteries.

2.Teaches Critical Thinking: Teaches students to separate empirical evidence from assumptions.

3.Connects to Real NASA Data: Provides direct student access to authentic space mission datasets.

4.Inspires New Explorers: Encourages the next generation of STEM researchers and engineers.

During development, our team carefully considered the balance between strict scientific rigor, specifically teaching students how to distinguish between planetary habitability and actual biological presence and broad accessibility, ensuring the learning experience remains engaging, intuitive and globally available for educators and learners worldwide.

**Tools Used & Development Approach**

**Gemini:** Used for vibe-coding the interactive gaming interface and designing the team logo image.

**Claude:** Used to refine the project details into formal academic language (which accounts for the high AI-detection score, though the raw, iterative concepts and text content were entirely our own).

**PowerPoint & Canva magic design:** Utilized with built-in AI features to craft visually eye-catching layouts and presentation slides.

**Google Vids:** Used for 240 seconds video narration and production.

**NASA Open-Access Data:** Integrated authentic research data, official mission records and planetary repositories directly from NASA’s scientific archives.
